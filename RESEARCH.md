# Global surf season atlas — editorial research notes

## Scope

This project compares 50 widely recognized surf destinations across 28 countries and territories. The list deliberately mixes iconic expert waves with globally important progression and longboard destinations, so the atlas remains useful beyond big-wave sightseeing.

## Monthly rating method

Each month receives an editorial trip-planning score from 1 to 5:

- **5 — Very good:** The spot’s favored swell window overlaps with generally favorable wind and usable seasonal weather.
- **4 — Good:** Strong odds, but with a meaningful weather, wind, consistency, or crowd tradeoff.
- **3 — Mixed:** Surfable and potentially rewarding, though shoulder-season variability is material.
- **2 — Poor:** Usually off-season, inconsistent, or compromised by weather and prevailing wind.
- **1 — Very poor:** The named break is rarely a sensible trip target in that month.

Wave quality and consistency carry the greatest weight. Prevailing wind, rainfall, air and water temperature, daylight, tropical-storm exposure, and access reliability are then applied as a travel-weather adjustment. Scores describe seasonal tendencies, not a forecast or a safety assessment. A rating of 5 at Pipeline or Teahupoʻo means prime conditions for surfers qualified for that break—not beginner-friendly conditions.

## Research base

The in-app source library links the regional references used to calibrate seasons, including official tourism boards for Hawaiʻi, Portugal, Costa Rica, El Salvador, Morocco, Barbados, Fiji, the Philippines, the Maldives, and New Zealand; local tourism bodies for Tofino, Jeffreys Bay, Bundoran, and Cornwall; and specialist spot guides from Surfline and Swell Archive.

The source set was reviewed on August 16, 2026. Exact conditions always depend on swell direction and period, wind, tide, local bathymetry, and recent sand movement. Travelers should use a current marine forecast and local guidance before entering the water.
